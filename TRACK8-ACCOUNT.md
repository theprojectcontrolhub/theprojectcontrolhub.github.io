# Track 8 — the author's account

Recorded 2026-08-19, in answer to the three questions in `TRACK8-KICKOFF.md`
§5. It is the first lived material to reach this project in eight years of
writing, and §5 says weeks 7 to 14 are the weeks that most need it.

`NOTES.md` §10 governs what happens to it: **the memory is the author's and is
not edited.** Section 1 below is therefore the account as given, in the
language it was given in, unaltered. Everything in section 2 onwards is mine —
what it changes, where it goes, and what is still missing. Nobody reading this
later should have trouble telling the two apart.

## 2. What is usable, and under which rule

`NOTES.md` §10 allows a composite scene provided the article says it is one,
and forbids putting a sentence in anybody's mouth that they did not say. It
also separates what happened from what was concluded. Sorting the account on
those lines:

**What happened — usable as fact, attributed to the author.**

- No integrated folder structure, no clean BOQ, no current Primavera
  programme, no standard progress tracking system on arrival.
- What existed instead: Excel files, daily reports, information from site, and
  data held by different individuals. Some of it arriving by WhatsApp.
- Much of the first week's information had to be requested from named
  individuals one at a time.
- Structures for daily manpower, equipment, production quantities and progress
  had to be built in Excel by the author.
- At one point roughly two hundred rows a day were being processed by hand,
  cross-checking weighbridge and delivery-note records against daily
  production reports and equipment records.
- No access to Primavera, and the planning system not fully under the author's
  control, while project control was the expected output.
- On the running project, a substantial part of the planning and control
  system existed in one person's knowledge, and continuity broke when that
  person left.
- Understanding the existing system required reverse engineering it from the
  spreadsheets and reports.

**What was concluded — the author's conclusions, and they are better than
anything I would have written.**

- *En temel problem aslında veri yokluğu değil, verinin sistematik ve
  güvenilir bir yapıda olmamasıydı.* The problem was never missing data. It
  was data with no reliable structure.
- *Bir kick-off toplantısında bir şeyin konuşulmuş olması, onun gerçekten
  sisteme dönüştüğü anlamına gelmiyor.* Being discussed at a kick-off is not
  the same as becoming a system.
- The gap that matters is between taking a decision in a meeting and turning
  the decision into something that can be operated.
- *Eğer sistem bir kişinin kafasındaysa, aslında sistem yoktur. Kişi vardır.*

**What is NOT usable.**

- There is no remembered kick-off scene. Question 2 came back as a pattern
  rather than an occasion, and the author says so directly. Nothing in the
  articles may present it as a specific meeting.
- The observation that deferred items vanish is given as *çok gördüm* — the
  author's own repeated observation. That is legitimate when attributed and
  becomes a frequency claim written from inference the moment it is
  generalised into "on most projects". Watch it.
- No client names, no real quantities beyond the two hundred rows the author
  volunteered, and that figure belongs to the author rather than to a worked
  example.

## 3. What it changes in what is already published

**It gives `lifecycle-week-8.html` the scene it was missing.** The first
morning is now on record as a progression rather than a set of events: opening
the existing spreadsheets and reporting files first, because there was no
single system showing the whole process; asking people where manpower,
production and equipment figures come from, which file is updated, which
report goes to whom; discovering by mid-morning that **the data existed and
the data flow did not**; and reaching lunchtime with the finding that there
was no working system to use, only a system to be understood first. By the end
of the day the question had changed from *how do I prepare this report* to
*which data can I trust, and where does it come from*.

That last shift is the article. It contains no invented dialogue and no
composite, so `NOTES.md` §10 is satisfied without a disclaimer.

**It corrects `lifecycle-week-9.html`, and the correction is substantive.**

Week 9 was written from structure and argued that an item leaves a kick-off in
one of three states — decided with an owner and a date, deferred to a named
person, or deferred to further discussion — and that only the third fails to
return. The author's account says that model is too generous at the top end:

> Ama bunların sorumlusu, formatı, deadline'ı ve kontrol mekanizması
> belirlenmezse birkaç hafta sonra herkes kendi yöntemine dönebiliyor.

An owner and a date are not sufficient. Without a **format** and a **control
mechanism**, a decided item decays back to everybody's own method within
weeks. So there is a fourth state that week 9 missed: decided, owned, dated,
and still not operable — which looks like success in the minutes and produces
nothing.

That is a better argument than the one I published, it comes from somebody who
watched it happen, and it is the difference §5 said this track most needed.
Week 9 was revised on it rather than left standing.

**And the second account extends the correction twice more.**

The four items become six, as a chain the author states outright:

> Owner → Format → Source → Deadline → Validation → Reporting

*Source* and *Validation* are the additions and neither is decorative. Source
is the link the first-morning account shows missing — data existed, its origin
did not. Validation is what makes a figure usable rather than merely present.

More importantly, the account supplies a **remedy**, which week 9 diagnosed
without. What survives is what has a clear owner *and* is attached to a daily
or weekly routine; the example given is daily reports prepared by named people
and sent to named people, which continue because they are wired into the day.
So the fourth state is not defeated by better minutes. It is defeated by
attaching the decision to something that already happens every day.

Weeks 9 and 10 both carry the four-item version and are updated to the chain.

## 5. Still missing

**Gap 2 is closed.** The first morning arrived with the second account and
week 8 now carries it.

**Gap 1 stays open, and is closed as far as it can be.** Asked again for a
kick-off as an occasion, the author answered:

> Burada dürüst olmak gerekirse toplantının tarihini, odadaki herkesin
> isimlerini veya bütün gündem maddelerini bugün birebir hatırlamıyorum. O
> yüzden olmayan detayları uydurmak istemem.

That is the right answer and it settles the question rather than leaving it
hanging. `NOTES.md` §10 forbids a scene containing a sentence nobody said, so
an occasion that is not remembered cannot be written, and nobody should ask
again. What did come back instead is more useful than a scene would have been:
the failure mode named as a broken chain, and the condition under which
decisions survive.

**One thing that would still add something**, if it ever surfaces without
being dug for: an instance where the chain *did* hold — a data flow that was
set up at the start and was still running two years later, and what was
different about it. The account says these exist and are the ones tied to a
daily routine. A single named example would have given week 14 something to
end on other than the failure.

**Week 14 was written without it, 2026-08-20, and closed differently.** Rather
than ending on a failure it ends on the phase: seven weeks, seven one-page
records, and the observation that producing them is the easy half while
attaching each to a cycle is the half that decides whether producing them was
worth anything. The author's own remedy — that what survives has an owner and
attaches to a daily or weekly rhythm — turns out to be a statement about
meetings, since the recurring meeting set is the only rhythm a project has.
That connection carried the week and the named example is no longer needed for
it. It would still improve week 39.
