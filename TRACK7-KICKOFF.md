## 2. Scope, and the two boundaries that matter

The track is about what changes when a project has more than one contract and
no single chain of command. Two boundaries keep it from swallowing other
tracks:

**It does not re-teach a method.** Concurrency belongs to Claims. Float
belongs to Schedule. Cost accounting belongs to Cost & Cash. Track 7 shows
where each stops applying and hands the technique back with a link, exactly
as Reporting does.

**It is not a delivery-models reference.** A catalogue of EPC, EPCM,
design-build, alliance, PPP and BOT is the thing `NOTES.md` closed twice
before — it collects rather than corrects. Delivery models appear only where a
specific one breaks a specific technique.

The fourth rule from §10 applies to every week here: **a week exists only if
it explains a failure.** "EPCM is a delivery model in which…" is a definition.
"There is no head contract, so the concurrency definition has nowhere to live"
is a failure, and it carries a week.

frequency.

## 4. The proposed 14 weeks

In `curriculum.js` as `TRACK7`, all `upcoming`, five phases.

**Who holds the scope**
1. The shape every track has assumed — one contract, one Engineer, one programme
2. How many contracts are there — the axis nobody teaches
3. EPCM — instructing people you have no contract with
4. The Engineer, multiplied — determination when every package has its own
5. Concurrency without a head contract — the Special Provisions that do not exist

**When the contractor is plural**
6. Joint ventures and consortia — one face, several sets of books
7. Alliancing, partnering and IPD — contracts built to suppress claims

**The critical path leaves the site**
8. Procurement on the critical path — when the path runs through an order

**Where two programmes meet**
9. The work in nobody's scope — the gap between two risk registers
10. Interface management as a function — owning a boundary, not reporting one
11. Access, sequencing and the delay that belongs to no one

**The number with more than one owner**
12. Progress and valuation collide — two methods, one monthly number
13. The cost that arrives from somebody else's books — another ledger, another cut-off
14. Document control at organisational scale — six firms, one transmittal

Known weaknesses in this list, to be settled early rather than discovered
in week 9:

- **Weeks 12–14 overlap Reporting.** Reporting week 25 already reconciles
  progress against valuation, week 14 already covers a ledger closing on
  somebody else's date, and week 13 already covers revision status. The Track 7
  versions must be the *multi-organisation* case: two firms both producing the
  figure correctly, six companies in one distribution. If they cannot be
  written that way they should be cut, and the track is 11 weeks.
- **Week 7 is the furthest from the thesis.** Alliancing is the opposite
  problem — contracts designed to suppress the claims machinery rather than
  fragment it. Defensible, and the first candidate for removal if the track
  needs to be shorter.
- **Week 4 may fold into week 3.**

## 9. The three editing rules that produced Reporting

They are in `NOTES.md` §10 in full. Repeated here because they matter most at
the start, when the temptation is to accept material that reads well.

1. **Separate what happened from what was concluded.** The memory is the
   author's and is not edited. A composite scene is allowed and must say it is
   one, but it may not contain a sentence nobody said.
2. **Do not mistake the symptom for the problem.** WhatsApp was not the
   problem; an untracked request was. A dashboard was not the problem; the data
   model was.
3. **Every idea has a home.** A good idea in the wrong week weakens two
   articles. During Reporting the same material tried three homes before it
   found the right one, and refusing it twice is why it landed well.

And the fourth, which decides whether a week exists at all:

4. **Every principle must explain a failure.** If the honest answer to "what
   goes wrong without this" is nothing specific, the week is not ready. Week 21
   of Reporting was left empty for two weeks on exactly this test and only
   written when the failure appeared.
